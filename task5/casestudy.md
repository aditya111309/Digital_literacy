Case Study: Phishing — The "Urgent Verification" Trap
Overview
Phishing is a form of social engineering where attackers deceive individuals into revealing sensitive information—such as login credentials or PINs—by masquerading as a trustworthy entity.

Step-by-Step Breakdown
The Bait: The attacker sends an email mimicking a reputable organization (e.g., a Bank or University IT).

The Hook: The message creates "false urgency," claiming an account will be suspended unless action is taken.

The Lead: The victim clicks a link that redirects to a fraudulent website identical to the official one.

The Capture: The victim enters their credentials, which the attacker records in real-time.

The Payoff: The attacker drains funds or steals data from the actual account.

Target Audience
While anyone is a target, attackers frequently focus on college students (via fake internship offers) and senior citizens who may be less familiar with URL spoofing.

Consequences
Victims face financial loss, identity theft, and long-term credit damage. For students, a compromised account can lead to the loss of academic records and the spread of malware across campus networks.
